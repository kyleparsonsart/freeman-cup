Nobody has held the Lassie yet. The first name goes on it October 10.

It's match play. Nobody counts total strokes. Each hole is its own contest. Win the hole and you're 1 up. A match ends when one side leads by more holes than remain, which is what 4 & 3 means.

Every match is one point. Win it, your team gets 1. All square after 18 is halved, half a point each. First team to 7½ of the 13½ takes the cup.

The Lassie is the trophy, a silver claret jug that lives with the winning side for a year and gets talked about for the other eleven months.

The states in between

Pairings locked (the night before). Same page, the placeholder rows in that day's tee sheet fill with real names. The "first tee" card starts naming the 8:00 group. Nothing else changes.

Trip morning, before first tee. Countdown hits zero and the strip flips to the live layout (0 to 0, Day 1 of 3). The forecast strip is real. The "first tee" card is replaced by four match cards in their pre-play state: names on each side, "8:00" in the middle where the status will be, no moments yet, scorecard accordion present but every cell a dot. That way the page's shape on Thursday at 7:30 is the same shape it has at 10:30.

Between rounds (Thursday night). Strip shows the running score. "Out on the course" shows "Next: Friday, The Commons, 8:10" with the forecast for that window, and Day 1's accordion is closed with its final score in the header.

After Saturday. Strip shows the final, the holder line gets its first name, the "Out on the course" section becomes "The final day" with all singles cards closed to their results, every scorecard still openable. The page then holds that state for a year and is the countdown again next September.

The holder line reads from one fact in the data, last year's winner, which is empty this year. Empty means the inaugural copy above; a value means "The Vikes hold the Lassie from 2026" and, in the roster headers, "Holders" and "Runners-up". Tee times and names here are placeholders.
